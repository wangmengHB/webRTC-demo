Welcome to 3D! 🌐
In 2D we used x (left/right) and y (up/down).

In 3D we add a third axis: z (towards you / away from you) — like depth into the screen!

Babylon.js needs 4 things to show anything:
Engine — runs the render loop (talks to the GPU)
Scene — the "world" that holds everything
Camera — your point of view
Light — without light, everything is pitch black!
🎮 Minecraft uses 3 axes too!In Minecraft: X = East/West, Y = Up/Down (bedrock to sky!), Z = North/South. Press F3 in Minecraft to see your X Y Z coordinates!

Your First 3D Block 🧊
In Babylon.js, 3D objects are called meshes. A box mesh is a cube!

We give it a material — like the paint on its surface.
MeshBuilder.CreateBox() makes a cube.
StandardMaterial lets us colour it.
mesh.position moves it in 3D space (x, y, z).

Try changing the colour values — they go from 0.0 to 1.0 (like a percentage of red, green, blue).
🧱 Everything in 3D is a mesh!A Minecraft block is the simplest possible mesh — 6 faces, 12 triangles. A complex game character might have 50,000 triangles!

A Floor of Blocks 🌍
Remember the nested loop from Mission 2? Same idea — but now we place blocks in 3D space!

position.x = left / right
position.z = forward / back (the new axis!)
position.y = up / down (height)

We also use a colour map — just like the level map from Mission 3, but in 3D!
⚡ Minecraft performance trick!Minecraft only draws block faces that are visible. A solid stone wall only needs its front face — the other 5 faces are hidden. This is called "face culling" and it's why Minecraft can show huge worlds!

Walk in Your World! 🚶
Now let's switch to a first-person camera — just like Minecraft!

Click the 3D view first to lock the mouse, then:
W / A / S / D or arrow keys → walk
Mouse → look around
E / Q or Space/Shift → up / down

We also add layers of blocks — grass on top, dirt below — using the Y axis!
🎥 How Minecraft's camera works!Minecraft stores the camera as a position (X,Y,Z) and two angles: yaw (turning left/right) and pitch (looking up/down). Babylon.js's UniversalCamera handles all of this automatically!

🏆 Place & Break Blocks!
The core Minecraft mechanic: left-click to break, right-click to place!

Babylon.js has ray casting — it shoots an invisible ray from the camera through the mouse and tells us exactly which block we're looking at. That's how Minecraft knows which block your crosshair is on!

Click the 3D view to lock cursor, then:
WASD to walk · Mouse to look
Left-click to break a block
Right-click to place a block
Keys 1–5 to switch block type
🔦 Ray casting — the magic behind Minecraft!Every frame, Minecraft shoots a ray from your eyes in the direction you're looking. Where it hits a block first = the block you're targeting. This same trick powers FPS aiming, 3D mouse picking, and even ray-traced graphics!
